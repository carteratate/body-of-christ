# 5.0. How Denzinger is used: entry map, concordance and costs

Research for corpus-cleanup item 5.0 (`docs/corpus-cleanup/P4-P5-republish-expand.md`, GitHub issue #194), done 15 November 2026. It follows Carter's decision of 15 November 2026 (Decision log "Denzinger in the expansion (5.0)"): option 2 for search, each entry placed where it belongs, only entries the corpus lacks in full, each passage carrying its D and DS numbers; and, if lawful, a Denzinger book in the reader built from the same passages.

This file holds the map of the book's entries against the corpus, the plan for a D-to-DS concordance, and the costs. The rights findings are kept privately, as the plan requires; Carter's decisions on them are in the Decision log.

No production store was queried and nothing was vendored. The book was read from the vendored Loreto reprint PDF's text layer (`datapipeline/sources/councils/sources-of-catholic-dogma/`, the file 1.2e uses) in a scratch folder: 749 of its 750 leaves (leaf 308, printed p. 277, will not load; it holds Trent, which the corpus has). The corpus side was read from `datapipeline/registry/works.json`, the council adapters' allowlists and the vendored sources.

## Result

- **The book holds about 2,400 numbered entries**: 2,313 plain numbers, roughly 80 lettered insertions (42a, 52a to 52e, 1890a and so on) and an appendix of seven (5000–5006). Creeds come first (1–40), then the documents of popes and councils in date order (41–2333), from Clement I to Munificentissimus Deus (1950).
- **About a third is already in the corpus in full.** 818 of the 2,340 numbers read are duplicates: the ecumenical councils (468, with 1.2e's Deferrari extracts), papal documents the corpus holds (350, with 1.3d's), and a few early papal letters quoted inside Fathers the corpus holds (Clement of Rome, Hippolytus, Cyprian's letters, Tertullian).
- **About 1,520 numbers are new or fill a gap**, in about 290 documents: 886 papal, 302 Holy Office, 189 regional councils and synods, 64 Biblical Commission, 37 other Roman congregations, 33 creeds, and 8 in three pieces missing from council documents the corpus has. 20 of them, whose originals were issued after 1930, are held for now; see "Held entries".
- **About half of what comes in is condemned propositions**: roughly 760 numbers (Baius's 79, Quesnel's 101, the Synod of Pistoia's 85, Molinos's 68, Lamentabili's 65, the Holy Office's moral lists of 1665 and 1679, Eckhart, Abelard, Rosmini and others). Under the rejected-voice rule (Decision log "Rejected voices (C-01)") they stay searchable with `voice = "rejected"` and a label naming whose position each is and who condemned it. That multiplies today's 150 rejected passages several times over.
- **Some entries have a fuller public-domain text** than Deferrari's extract: Serdica's canons and the African canons (Carthage 397 and 418) in Percival (NPNF2 vol. 14, vendored), Dionysius of Rome and Julius I in NPNF2 vol. 4 (Athanasius, vendored), and 18 documents of Leo XIII to Pius XI whose whole English is on vatican.va.
- **No open D-to-DS concordance exists online.** The citable one is printed in Hünermann's editions (DH; the Latin–English 43rd edition, Ignatius Press 2012). Building ours means recording about 2,400 pairs from a printed copy and checking them; see section 3.
- **Costs are small in money and large in reading time.** About 130,000 to 140,000 words of decree text, 700 to 1,100 passages, roughly $5 of model clean-up, a few cents of embeddings, and about 4 MB of database and 8 MB of Qdrant. The work is in setting up up to 290 per-document OCR works and checking roughly 600 fixes against the page images.

## 1. What the book is

*The Sources of Catholic Dogma* (B. Herder, 1957) is Roy J. Deferrari's English of the 30th edition of Denzinger's *Enchiridion Symbolorum* (Herder, Freiburg, 1954, revised by Karl Rahner). Its margin numbers are the "D" numbers (sometimes "Denz." or "DB", for Bannwart, who introduced them in 1908). Since the 32nd edition (1963, Schönmetzer) the book has been renumbered: the "DS" numbers modern documents cite (the Catechism's footnotes, for example), kept by Hünermann's editions (DH) up to 3997.

Each document opens with a heading and a bracketed source line ("[Condemned in a decree of the Holy Office, March 4, 1679]", "[From the Encyclical "Quanta cura," Dec. 8, 1864]"), and the running head names the pope or council. The map below was built from those lines, the General Index (pp. vii–xxix) and the margin numbers.

### Notes on the map

- **Duplicates hidden inside other documents.** Leo's Tome (143–144) is in Chalcedon's document; Cyril's second letter (111a) in Ephesus's; Unam Sanctam, Ubi Primum (1824), Mirari Vos, Inter Praecipuas, Qui Pluribus, Ineffabilis Deus, Quanta Cura (1864), the Syllabus, Etsi Multa, Quod Nunquam, Immortale Dei, Libertas, Rerum Novarum, Providentissimus Deus, Mirae Caritatis, Pascendi, Spiritus Paraclitus, Quas Primas, Casti Connubii, Quadragesimo Anno, Divini Redemptoris, Mystici Corporis, Divino Afflante Spiritu, Mediator Dei, Humani Generis and Munificentissimus Deus are all held whole. The early letters quoted by Fathers (Clement I, Callistus in Tertullian's On Modesty, Cornelius and Stephen in Cyprian's letters, Zephyrinus in Hippolytus) are held inside those works; On Modesty is subject to rule A.
- **Whole texts already vendored.** Percival (`councils/npnf214.xml`) prints Serdica's canons and the African code, which includes Carthage 397's canon of Scripture and the canons of 418 on grace; P1a left them out as not ecumenical. NPNF2 vol. 4 (`church-fathers/npnf204.xml`) holds Athanasius's *De decretis* and *Apologia*, with Dionysius of Rome's letter (48–51) and Julius I's (57a); only On the Incarnation is in the corpus today. Taking the whole text from these instead of Deferrari's extract is a question for Carter (below).
- **Whole English on vatican.va.** Eighteen documents of Leo XIII to Pius XI (Quod Apostolici, Arcanum, Diuturnum, Humanum Genus, Sapientiae Christianae, Pastoralis Officii, Octobri Mense, Satis Cognitum, Apostolicae Curae, Testem Benevolentiae, Ad Diem Illum, Vehementer Nos, Communium Rerum, Ubi Arcano, Studiorum Ducem, Infinita Dei Misericordia, Divini Cultus, Divini Illius Magistri) are on vatican.va in English, which would come in as whole documents under the vatican.va rules (Decision log "LEV texts from vatican.va") rather than as Deferrari's extracts.
- **The anathemas against Origen (203–211).** Denzinger prints them under Vigilius as Justinian's of 543. 1.2c left Percival's fifteen anathemas out of Constantinople II (Decision log "Percival's extra texts (1.2c)"). Placed as a regional synod's they would not be filed as the council's teaching.
- **Not decree text.** Bautain's theses (1622–1627) are propositions a theologian signed; Nicholas of Autrecourt's (553–569) are errors he recanted before a papal legate; the errors of Peter of Bonageta (576–580) were condemned by inquisitors. Each would say so in its note.
- **Held entries.** About 20 numbers whose original was issued after 1930 and which the corpus does not hold: the Holy Office of 1931, 1940, 1944 (two) and 1949; the Biblical Commission of 1933 and 1948; Ad Catholici Sacerdotii (1935), Firmissimam Constantiam (1937), Summi Pontificatus (1939), Pius XII's addresses of 1941 and 1949, and Sacramentum Ordinis (1947). Carter's decision on them is in the Decision log.
- **Accuracy.** The map is by document, from the book's headings and source lines; every "Duplicate" and "Fills a gap" was checked against the corpus's sources or registry, but each new document's exact extent is settled when its OCR work is written. The full table is the appendix.

## 3. A concordance from D to DS

**No open, machine-readable concordance was found.** Searches in English and German, the Internet Archive, and developer indexes turned up none; Logos/Verbum's commercial *Sources of Catholic Dogma* prints the DS number beside each entry. The citable source is printed:

- **Denzinger–Hünermann** (37th German edition onward; the 43rd, Latin–English, edited by Robert Fastiggi and Anne Englund Nash, Ignatius Press 2012, ISBN 978-0-89870-746-5) has an appendix concording its numbers with the editions before 1963, and prints the old number beside each entry.
- Schönmetzer's DS editions (32nd to 36th, 1963–1976) print the old D number in the inner margin of every entry.

The DS numbers up to 3997 are the same in DS and DH, so either gives the modern citation.

**What building ours takes:**

1. A printed DH (a library copy, or the 43rd edition at about $100) or a DS.
2. Record each pair: D number (with its letter) to DS number, about 2,400 rows, as a data file beside the registry (`datapipeline/registry/denzinger_numbers.json` or similar), with the source edition and page.
3. Check it mechanically: DS numbers rise with D numbers inside each document; every D number in Deferrari appears once; every pair's DS falls inside its document's DS range.
4. Check it by hand: about 100 pairs against the DS edition's own margins, and every DS number the Catechism cites for a text we hold against our pairing.
5. Where one D number splits into several DS numbers (DS often divides an entry), record the range; where a D entry has no DS counterpart (rare: DS dropped a few texts), say so.

Allow a day of recording and a day of checks. The pairs are what each passage carries as its citations (`D 1092`, `DS 2001–2005`), whether placed by option 2 or already held.

## 4. Costs

### Text and passages

- **About 166,000 words** in the text layer for the entries that come in, about 130,000 to 140,000 of them decree text once Denzinger's notes go.
- **700 to 1,100 passages.** 1.2e's Deferrari works averaged about 260 words a passage (10,800 words in 41 passages); many Denzinger documents here are a few lines long, and condemned lists pack into passages of the usual size, one proposition to a paragraph, as with Wyclif's and Hus's articles.

### OCR and clean-up (the 1.2f tool, as 1.2e used it)

- **Inputs exist.** The vendored PDF and its text layer go through 1.2e's `ocr/pdf.py` path; nothing new is downloaded.
- **Setup per document.** Each document is its own work (`datapipeline/ocr/works/deferrari-*.json`) with its pages and a pattern for each unit's first and last line, as 1.2e's seven works do. That is up to about 290 works, fewer if a pope's short letters share one. Pages printed in parallel columns (Greek and Latin, as Constantinople IV's canons) need the column-aware layout; among the new entries these are mostly the early creeds and synods.
- **Fixes.** 1.2e tracked 48 fixes in 10,800 words (about 4.5 per 1,000) after reading against the page images. At that rate, about 600 here. The corrigenda sheet at the front of the reprint corrects six places among the new entries (74, 257, 296, 1096, 2164, and p. 194 in John XXII's condemnation of Marsilius) and 2302, which is held for now; its other corrections (111a, 148, 218, 219, 691, 703, 797, 818, 1839) fall in texts the corpus already has.
- **Model spend.** Claude Sonnet 5.5 at $2 and $10 per million tokens, about 1 million tokens in and 200,000 out: about $4 to $5 in all, under the $2 ceiling per work.
- **Reading time** is the real cost: checking each work's sample against the page images, as 1.2f requires, at 1.2e's pace.

### Embeddings and storage

- **Embeddings:** about 180,000 tokens with `text-embedding-3-large` at $0.13 per million: a few cents.
- **Supabase:** about 3.5 KB a passage (5.2's estimate), so about 3 to 4 MB.
- **Qdrant:** about 7.3 KB a passage (vector and payload), so about 5 to 8 MB, under 2% of the live collection. A 2.2w publish briefly holds a second collection beside the live one, as every P5 publish does; this adds nothing particular to that peak.

### The book view

No new passages: it orders the placed and the held passages by D number. It needs the concordance and every duplicate's D number attached to the corpus passage that holds it (about 820 numbers mapped to existing passages, by hand, document by document). Reader work: a Denzinger "book" that lists entries by number and opens each in its own document, or shows them in sequence. Short headings come from Deferrari's General Index.

## 5. Questions for Carter

Asked on 15 November 2026; answers below.

1. Whether the book view is lawful, on what basis, and whether to build it.
2. Where each kind of entry goes (the table in section 2).
3. What happens to the post-1930 entries the corpus lacks.
4. Whether a whole public-domain or vatican.va text replaces Deferrari's extract where one exists.

### Answers (Carter, 16 November 2026)

Every recommendation was accepted. The Decision log rows are "Denzinger book view (5.0)", "Where Denzinger's entries go (5.0)", "Denzinger entries issued after 1930 (5.0)" and "Fuller texts over Deferrari's extracts (5.0)".

1. **Book view: built.** Passages carry decree text only; the book lists entries by D number in Denzinger's order with short headings, and uses no Denzinger notes, introductions, source lines or Systematic Index (the conditions the private rights memo sets).
2. **Placement: as proposed** in section 2.
3. **Post-1930 entries: held** until each is public domain in the US (1 January 2027 for the 1931 Holy Office decree, through 2045 for the last); where vatican.va has the whole document in English, it comes in whole under the vatican.va rules.
4. **Fuller texts win:** Percival's Serdica and African canons, NPNF's Dionysius of Rome and Julius I, and the 18 vatican.va documents come in whole, carrying their D and DS numbers. Deferrari supplies only what nothing fuller covers.

## Method

- The text layer was extracted with pypdfium2 in a scratch virtual environment. Margin numbers were found at line starts and ends and kept where they form a rising sequence (2,280 of 2,313 plain numbers and 68 lettered ones recovered). Source lines were matched by their bracket and wording ("Holy Office", "S. Off.", "Biblical Commission", "Congregation", "Index").
- Each document was classed by hand from the General Index, the source lines and the running heads, then checked against `datapipeline/registry/works.json`, the Percival allowlist (`ingest/councils_percival.py`), and the vendored council and Fathers sources.
- Words per number are counted between consecutive margin numbers and include notes.
- Not searched: HathiTrust (refuses this server, as in R8 and R9).
