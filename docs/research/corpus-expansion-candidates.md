# Corpus expansion candidates

Checked 16 September 2026 against `datapipeline/SOURCES.md` and the current `datapipeline/sources/*/manifest.json` files. These are candidates for research and rights clearance, not publication instructions.

## What the repository already has

TheoCorpus has a Catholic Bible translation (WEB-C), the Catechism, the *Summa*, selected Church Fathers and medieval works, Latin canon law, 20 ecumenical councils plus all 16 Vatican II documents, and papal texts. The local manifests list 131 items under `encyclicals`, 30 apostolic exhortations, 14 other papal documents, 36 council documents, ten Church Fathers source volumes, and four medieval source files. `datapipeline/SOURCES.md` said the encyclical set had 18 works; that count was stale and was corrected to 131 on 17 September 2026. The `encyclicals` manifest also includes *Evangelii Nuntiandi* and *Evangelii Gaudium*, both apostolic exhortations rather than encyclicals, so their document types should be checked before that collection is used as a precise filter. (*Laudato Si'* is also present and is correctly classified. *Laudate Deum* — an apostolic exhortation, and named here in an earlier draft — is not vendored in any collection.) [Local inventory](../../datapipeline/SOURCES.md). The manifests themselves are not in the repository — `datapipeline/sources/` is gitignored and populated by `scripts/vendor_sources.py`, so these counts are reproducible only on a tree that has been vendored.

## Ranked gaps

1. **Doctrinal dicastery documents.** Add a collection for declarations, instructions, and notes of the Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith and its predecessor. The existing categories center on popes and councils, leaving important direct treatments of contested doctrinal and moral questions out of search. Good starting documents are [*Dominus Iesus*](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_con_cfaith_doc_20000806_dominus-iesus_en.html) on Christ and the Church and [*Dignitas Infinita*](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/cfaith/documents/rc_ddf_doc_20240402_dignitas-infinita_en.html) on human dignity. Neither title appears in the current manifests. Store issuing body, document type, date, and numbered sections so search results do not imply that all texts have the same authority.

2. **A systematic social teaching reference.** The [*Compendium of the Social Doctrine of the Church*](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/pontifical_councils/justpeace/documents/rc_pc_justpeace_doc_20060526_compendio-dott-soc_en.html) supplies a numbered, topical synthesis spanning the person, family, work, economy, political life, environment, peace, and the Church's social mission. Individual papal texts already cover many of these themes, but the compendium gives users a direct entry point and references back to those texts. Its [analytical index](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/pontifical_councils/justpeace/documents/rc_pc_justpeace_doc_20060526_analytical-index_en.html) could support browsing as well as retrieval.

3. **Liturgy and worship guidance.** The [*General Instruction of the Roman Missal*](https://www.vatican.va/content/dam/wss/roman_curia/congregations/ccdds/documents/rc_con_ccdds_doc_20030317_ordinamento-messale_en.html), [*Redemptionis Sacramentum*](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/ccdds/documents/rc_con_ccdds_doc_20040423_redemptionis-sacramentum_en.html), and [*Directory on Popular Piety and the Liturgy*](https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/ccdds/documents/rc_con_ccdds_doc_20020513_vers-direttorio_en.html) answer practical questions about Mass, Eucharistic discipline, devotions, and how devotions relate to liturgy. A `liturgy-guidance` collection should keep norms distinct from the prayer texts themselves. Full Missal, Lectionary, and Liturgy of the Hours texts need separate rights review.

4. **Spiritual classics and more of the Fathers.** The current medieval set is four source files, and the patristic set omits many major works. For prayer and spiritual formation, consider Teresa of Avila's [*Interior Castle*](https://ccel.org/ccel/teresa/castle2/castle2.iv.html), John of the Cross's [*Dark Night of the Soul*](https://www.ccel.org/ccel/john_cross/dark_night.iv.html), and Thérèse of Lisieux's [*Story of a Soul*](https://ccel.org/ccel/therese/autobio.pdf). For patristic theology and pastoral care, consider Basil's [*On the Holy Spirit*](https://ccel.org/ccel/schaff/npnf208.toc.html), Cyril of Jerusalem's catechetical lectures and Gregory Nazianzen's orations in [NPNF2-07](https://ccel.org/ccel/schaff/npnf207/npnf207.iii.vi.html), and Gregory the Great's [*Pastoral Rule*](https://ccel.org/ccel/schaff/npnf212/npnf212.toc.html). These should carry author, era, work, book, and chapter metadata; an old translation or an author's opinion should not be displayed as current Church law.

5. **Eastern Catholic canon law.** The current `canon-law` source is the Latin Church's 1983 code. The Holy See publishes the [*Codex Canonum Ecclesiarum Orientalium* in Latin](https://www.vatican.va/content/john-paul-ii/la/apost_constitutions/documents/hf_jp-ii_apc_19901018_index-codex-can-eccl-orient.html), which governs the Eastern Catholic Churches. The Vatican archive presents the full Eastern code in Latin, while the [Canon Law Society of America sells a Latin-English edition updated in 2024](https://clsa.org/store/viewproduct.aspx?id=24446865). Do not assume the English translation can be indexed or republished without a license. Even a licensed addition should distinguish Eastern and Latin canons, and account for later amendments listed in the [Holy See's canon-law archive](https://www.vatican.va/archive/cdc/index.htm).

6. **A short question-and-answer catechism.** The Vatican's [*Compendium of the Catechism of the Catholic Church*](https://www.vatican.va/archive/compendium_ccc/documents/archive_2005_compendium-ccc_en.html) offers concise question-and-answer entries keyed to the full Catechism. It is useful for direct answers, though lower priority because the full Catechism is already present. Preserve links to the corresponding full Catechism paragraphs.

## Rights and source handling

Public web access does not grant permission to copy an entire work into a search index. The [Vatican portal's legal notes](https://www.vatican.va/content/vatican/en/legal-notes.html) say that collection and reproduction beyond personal use require prior written authorization. The [USCCB's Bible permissions page](https://www.usccb.org/offices/new-american-bible/permissions) identifies the Confraternity of Christian Doctrine as the copyright holder for the NABRE and describes licensing requirements; this matters if a second Bible translation or Lectionary is considered. The [CCEL copyright policy](https://www.ccel.org/about/copyright.html) says most base editions are public domain in the United States, but CCEL editions can contain protected material and CCEL asks for permission to republish works or use them commercially. Check each translation and edition, plus intended use, before ingestion.
