# Scan-only works: where clean text exists

Date 2026-09-28. Scope is the 49 rows in section 2.4 (A2) of `2026-09-28-theologians-spiritual-writers-candidates.md` whose Source column says "scan", minus row 17 (Newman, *Parochial and Plain Sermons*, now excluded). That leaves 48 works. Two council replacements are added at the end.

Rules applied, per the coordinator's update. Peers translations do not count, so for John of the Cross and Teresa only David Lewis (1864, 1871 and his later revisions) and Stanbrook texts dated 1930 or earlier count. For Tanquerey the printing matters. A 1930 printing is needed; the 1932 Belgian printing is likely under restored US copyright.

"Verified" means I opened the page or file and read it. "Inferred" means I am relying on a search result, a title, or a site's own label without reading the text.

## Summary

- **Clean full text found for 19 of 48 works.** Carter was right that many are not scan-only. Four of the 19 carry a flag, set out in the "Flags on the clean set" list below.
- **Partial for 6.** Some of the work is clean, the rest is scan or unproofread OCR.
- **OCR only for 23.** Nothing better than Internet Archive or Google OCR turned up.

The biggest finds are two sites the earlier memo did not check.

- **ecatholic2000.com** now holds clean HTML of Bernard's *Sermons on the Canticle* (Mount Melleray, 1920, both volumes), *On Consideration* (Lewis 1908), *Grace and Free Will* (Williams 1920), the complete *Catena Aurea* including Luke and John, Neale's *Moral Concordances*, Parker's Dionysius, Robinson's *Writings of St Francis*, and the *Little Flowers*. The site claims copyright over all of it (see Notes).
- **sensusfidelium.com** holds complete, clean chapter-by-chapter HTML of Bonaventure's *Life of St Francis* (Salter 1904), Bellarmine's *Eternal Happiness of the Saints*, Liguori's *Preparation for Death*, and Luis of Granada's *Sinner's Guide*.

Important works that remain OCR-only:

- Teresa, *Foundations* (Lewis), *Letters* (Stanbrook) and *Way of Perfection* (Dalton, or Stanbrook 1911). Every clean Teresa text found online is Peers.
- John of the Cross, *Ascent*, *Dark Night*, *Living Flame* in Lewis. Only the *Spiritual Canticle* exists clean in Lewis.
- Tanquerey, *The Spiritual Life*. The only scans found are of the 1930 printing, which is the one needed, but no clean text exists.
- Marmion, *Christ the Life of the Soul*. *Christ in His Mysteries* is only partly online as clean text.
- Gertrude, both books.
- Aquinas, the Rawes catechetical sermons (*Lord's Prayer*, *Commandments*).
- More, *Dialogue of Comfort* in the Everyman text.
- Knox, *The Belief of Catholics*.
- Chesterton, *The Thing*.
- Fisher, *English Works*.
- Faber, Lallemant, Möhler, Scheeben, and William of St Thierry.

Flags on the clean set:

- #12 *Spiritual Combat*. The clean Wikisource text is a Rivingtons (London) 1875 edition, not Burns 1846. Rivingtons was an Anglican house, so check it against a Catholic edition for edits.
- #23 *Little Flowers*. The clean text is the Hudleston revision of the "first English translation" (Burns Oates 1926), not Okey.
- #41 *Summa contra Gentiles*. Aquinas.cc shows the same English Dominican (Shapcote) translation, but as the Aquinas Institute edited it (2018/2019), and it states no licence.
- #46 Catherine of Genoa. The clean CCEL text is a 1907 New York printing of *Life and Doctrine*, probably not the 1858 Burns & Lambert translation.

Council replacements:

- **Schaff, *Creeds of Christendom* vol. 2**, including Vatican I, is clean on CCEL and marked "Proofed".
- **Schroeder, *Disciplinary Decrees*** is partial. About 4% of its pages are proofread on Wikisource, and Fordham's sourcebook has three councils (Lateran I, II and IV) in Schroeder's translation.

## Notes

**Method.**

- Parsed the full Project Gutenberg catalogue (pg_catalog.csv) for every author and title.
- Queried the Wikisource search API in the main, Page and Index namespaces.
- Read the Wikisource author page for each author.
- Counted proofreading status per Index through the ProofreadPage API.
- Pulled CCEL author pages and plain-text file headers.
- Fetched all 55 ecatholic2000.com sitemaps (26,611 URLs). Bernard's pages exist but are not in the sitemaps, so the sitemap undercounts that site.
- Listed Sensus Fidelium's meditation sections.
- Queried Faded Page's search endpoint by title.
- Ran web searches for each title.

To check quality, I read sample passages. I also ran a dictionary check that flags lower-case words missing from `/usr/share/dict/words`. On ecatholic2000's Bernard and Catena pages, every flagged word was a real inflection or British spelling ("judgement", "favour"), which points to typed or corrected text.

**What I did not check.**

- PGDP (Distributed Proofreaders) projects in progress. Its project search needs a login.
- HathiTrust and Google Books full text. Both are OCR by definition.
- Logos and Verbum. They sell some of these texts, e.g. Schroeder ([Logos](https://www.logos.com/product/16635/disciplinary-decrees-of-the-general-councils-text-translation-and-commentary?ssi=0)) and SCG ([Logos](https://www.logos.com/product/5206/summa-contra-gentiles)), but they are paid and licensed.
- Hugging Face and GitHub datasets.
- Paid Kindle reprints.

**Rights on the transcriptions.**

- **ecatholic2000.** Every Bernard page carries "COPYRIGHT © 2024 BY eCatholic2000. ALL RIGHTS RESERVED." The general footer reads "Copyright ©1999-2026 Wildfire Fellowship, Inc all rights reserved" ([example page](https://www.ecatholic2000.com/stjohn/ascent.shtml)). Its robots.txt allows everything ([robots.txt](https://www.ecatholic2000.com/robots.txt)). The underlying translations are all 1930 or earlier, and a faithful transcription of a public-domain text adds no new US copyright. Still, the site says what it says. Same caution as the Newman Reader note in the candidates memo: ask, or re-derive from the scan using ecatholic2000 as a proofreading aid.
- **Sensus Fidelium.** Only a "D-Sign: ©2023" theme credit was seen. Verified on the pages read.
- **CCEL.** File headers state "Rights: Public Domain" where present (Dionysius *Works*, Schaff vol. 2, Stevens *Dialogue*). Verified. Headers for the Canticle and Catherine files carry no rights line.
- **Wikisource and Gutenberg.** Wikisource tags works {{PD-old}} or similar. Gutenberg texts carry the PG licence.
- **aquinas.cc.** No licence statement found. The Aquinas Institute sells the same text in print. Ask before using.

**Spot-check caveats.**

- ecatholic2000's *True Devotion*, *Writings of St Francis* and *Little Flowers* were identified from headers and tables of contents, not read. Treat quality there as inferred.
- Sensus Fidelium does not name its editions. The *Preparation for Death*, *Eternal Happiness* and *Sinner's Guide* attributions are inferred from wording and should be checked against the scans before ingest.

**Row 1.** For John of the Cross, the only clean Lewis text found is the *Spiritual Canticle*, in the 1909 Baker printing with Zimmerman's introduction ([CCEL](https://ccel.org/ccel/j/john_cross/canticle/cache/canticle.txt)). Wikisource lists a Lewis *Living Flame* (1919) but the page does not exist ([author page](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Author:John_of_the_Cross)).

**Row 49, Tanquerey printing.** Two items are both catalogued as 1930 by Internet Archive (verified in item metadata), which fits the requirement:

- [spirituallife0000atan](https://archive.org/details/spirituallife0000atan), "Society Of St. John The Evangelist; Desclee & Co."
- [MN41530ucmf_5](https://archive.org/details/MN41530ucmf_5), "Tournai (Belgium)", xlviii, 750, 21 p.

Confirm the date on the title page and verso before use, since IA dates are sometimes taken from the first edition. The 1932 printing was not found online.
