Theme Width Cautions

The idea: three voices under the text

1. Read with care: TheoCorpus speakingOur editorial judgment about this passage, addressed to the reader. It is the only thing under the text that asks you to act differently (cite it carefully, not take it as the whole, not take it as binding). So it is the only one that is easy to see: full-strength text at the same size as the record line below it, on a quiet panel, opened by a word saying which kind of caution it is.
2. About this text: the recordFacts that identify the text, as a bibliography would: what kind of document, how it survives, whose translation, whether it came from a scanned page. Neutral reference data you look up rather than read. One grey line, items separated by dots, no label.
3. Required notice: the rights holder speakingSomeone else's words, printed because we must, verbatim. Not information for the reader. The quietest thing on the card: fine print, set apart by a hairline, last.

Kinds of caution (slot 1)

Each caution says which question it answers. The four words are the whole vocabulary; every note in the corpus fits one.

◐IncompleteIs this the whole text? Abridged, a summary, an extract, part left untranslated.
≈WordingAre these the original words? Another chronicler's version, an unofficial English, a passage in Latin.
✎AuthorshipWho really wrote it? An attribution doubt the header cannot hold, a later addition by another hand.
§AuthorityDoes it carry the weight it seems to? Not enacted, not adopted by the Church, superseded.

Why not the gold accent for cautions: the Encyclicals cards are tinted gold already (see Ineffabilis Deus below), and gold is the app's colour for links and buttons. The panel uses the text colour instead, so it reads the same on every collection's tint.

Decided

Variant A, the labelled panel, for cautions: the kind word needs no legend and a screen reader reads it. The record as one plain grey line. The required notice as fine print under a hairline. Three looks, never mixed: a line that is not a caution and not a notice is part of the record (Decision log, "Notes under an opened passage (4.4)" and "Look of the notes (4.4)").
